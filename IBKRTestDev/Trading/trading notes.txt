The NYSE is an auction market while NASDAQ is a dealer market.

Acts as a Broker (or Agent):
When a brokerage executes orders on behalf of its clients, buying or selling securities for them, it's acting as a broker. 

Acts as a Dealer (or Principal):
When a brokerage trades for its own account, buying and selling securities to make a profit, it's acting as a dealer. 

Facilitates Trading:
Broker-dealers help connect buyers and sellers of securities in the market. 

Market Makers:
On Nasdaq, many broker-dealers serve as market makers, continually updating their bid (what they're willing to buy for) and ask (what they're willing to sell for) prices. 

Nasdaq and Broker-Dealers:

    Dealer Market:
    Nasdaq is a dealer market, meaning that trading happens electronically, with many dealers competing to provide prices for securities. 

    Market Makers:
    The broker-dealers who act as market makers on Nasdaq are essential to the market's liquidity and efficient operation. 

    Regulation:
    Broker-dealers operating on Nasdaq are subject to regulation by the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA), a self-regulatory organization. 

    In essence, broker-dealers are the firms and individuals that make the Nasdaq market function, by buying and selling securities, and by providing liquidity and prices for investors.

ISIN is a 12-digit code most commonly used throughout the world to identify stocks 
and bonds. ISIN is an acronym for International Securities Identification Number. 

ISINs are most commonly used for public listings and IPOs and are a requirement 
for most stock exchanges worldwide.

In stocks, "ARCA" refers to the NYSE Arca, an electronic securities exchange where 
stocks and options are traded. It was previously known as Archipelago Exchange 
(ArcaEX) and merged with the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) in 2006. NYSE Arca 
now operates as a subsidiary of the NYSE Group.

OHLC stands for Open-High-Low-Close, and it's a common way to visually represent 
price movements of a financial instrument over time. Each vertical line on an OHLC 
chart shows the price range (high and low) and the opening and closing prices for 
a specific period (e.g., one day or one hour).
